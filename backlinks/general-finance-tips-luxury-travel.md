**Title:** Why does a yacht charter quote end up 40-60% higher than the base fee once the trip is over?

Something I see people miss again and again with jet, villa and yacht bookings: the brochure rate is not the budget. On a Mediterranean motor yacht, the base fee often covers just the boat and crew. On top of that you usually pay an Advance Provisioning Allowance (often 25-40% of the base) for fuel, food, drinks, marina fees and port dues. Then there is VAT, which depends on the country and the cruising area. Then a crew gratuity, usually 10-20%. If you cruise hard, fuel can run past the APA and you settle the difference at the end.

Jets follow the same pattern: repositioning legs, overnight crew fees, de-icing, catering, landing fees and federal excise tax on US flights. Villas add security deposits, staff gratuities, chef food costs and local tourist taxes.

The habit that fixes this is boring: before you sign, ask for an estimate of the total cash that will leave your account, and keep a 15-20% buffer on top of it. Write down every line that isn't included and put a number next to it.

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What hidden costs should I budget for when chartering a luxury yacht, private jet or villa?

The short answer: budget for the total cash that will leave your account, not the rate on the brochure. With high-end rentals the advertised price is usually a base fee, and the extras are predictable once you know where to look.

Yachts: Under the common Mediterranean terms, the base fee covers the vessel and crew. On top of that you pay an Advance Provisioning Allowance (APA), typically 25-40% of the base, which funds fuel, food, drinks, berthing and port fees. Any unspent APA comes back to you, but heavy cruising can push fuel past it, and you settle that shortfall at the end. VAT depends on the flag and the cruising area and can be significant. A crew gratuity of 10-20% of the base is standard. A 35% APA on a €180,000 charter is €63,000 before VAT or tips.

Private jets: Ask whether the quote includes repositioning (empty) legs, crew overnight and per diem charges, de-icing, catering, landing and handling fees, and, on US flights, federal excise tax. Fuel surcharges can change between quote and departure.

Villas: Expect a refundable security deposit, staff gratuities, the chef's food and drink costs billed separately, utility overages at some properties, and local tourist taxes.

Three habits prevent the nasty surprises:

1. Ask for an all-in estimate in writing, with every exclusion listed.
2. Put a number next to each exclusion, even if it's a rough one.
3. Keep a 15-20% contingency on top of the total, and track the APA or incidentals account during the trip, not after.
