## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How do I budget for a luxury yacht charter and avoid surprise costs?

The most important thing to understand is that the base charter fee is not your cost. It is the starting point. On a week-long Mediterranean charter, the final invoice can easily land 25 to 30 percent above the base fee, and I have seen it go further.

Here is what typically sits on top of the base fee:

1. Advance provisioning allowance (APA). This is usually 25 to 35 percent of the base fee, paid upfront. It covers fuel, food, drinks, dockage and other expenses. Whatever you do not spend is returned, but plan as if you will spend most of it.

2. Fuel. Cruising speed changes consumption dramatically. A longer itinerary with more distance at higher speed can multiply the number.

3. Marina and berthing fees. Popular ports in high season are expensive, and some charge by vessel length.

4. VAT. It varies by country and by where the charter starts and ends. Ask your broker for the exact rate for your itinerary.

5. Crew gratuity. The customary range is 10 to 20 percent of the base fee, paid in cash at the end.

My practical advice is to set a hard all-in ceiling before you look at any listings or photos. Photos are what make people stretch. Then ask your broker for a written estimate covering every line above, and keep a buffer of at least 25 percent over the base fee.

Finally, negotiate the itinerary, not just the rate. Shorter distances and off-peak ports often save more than a discount on the base fee.
