- Add crew gratuity (usually 10 to 20 percent) before comparing quotes

The separate account mattered more than I expected. When you can see the balance, it gets harder to talk yourself into upgrades.

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How should I budget for a luxury yacht charter so there are no surprise costs?

Start with the year, not the trip. Most charter budgets fail because people price the boat and treat everything else as extras. On a crewed yacht, the base charter fee is often only 60 to 70 percent of what you actually spend.

Here is how I build a charter budget now:

1. Set a yearly luxury travel number first. Decide what you will spend on all jets, villas and yachts this year, then move that money into a separate account. Book trips against that balance, not against optimism.

2. Treat the APA as a fixed cost. The Advance Provisioning Allowance covers fuel, food, drinks, marina fees and port charges. Plan for 25 to 35 percent of the base fee, and more if you plan long passages or order premium wine.

3. Add gratuity before comparing quotes. Crew tips usually run 10 to 20 percent of the base rate. A cheaper boat with a larger crew can end up costing more.

4. Price the logistics. That means flights or jet legs, transfers, VAT where it applies, cruising taxes and any hotel nights before the charter.

5. Keep a 10 percent buffer in the same account. Weather days, extra fuel and the dinner ashore you did not plan for all come out of it.

I learned this the hard way when my APA ran out on day four in the BVI and the week landed at $38,000. I wrote up the full method, including how to work out a year of luxury spending backwards, here: https://siavashsed.github.io/crestcharter/general-finance-tips-budget-backwards/

The key habit is keeping the money separate. When your luxury travel money lives in its own account, you see the true cost before you book, and a broker asking for a top-up never comes as a shock.
