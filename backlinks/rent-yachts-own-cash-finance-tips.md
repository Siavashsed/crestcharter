**Title:** Why I charter a yacht every summer instead of buying one, and why the cash buffer matters more than the boat

I've chartered a motor yacht most summers for years, and in all that time I've owned almost nothing that floats. People assume that's a lifestyle choice. Mostly it's arithmetic.

The common industry rule of thumb is that a yacht costs around 10% of its purchase price every year to run: crew, berthing, insurance, maintenance, fuel. You pay that whether you use the boat for three weeks or thirty. If you only want it for a few weeks each summer, chartering means you pay for the weeks you actually use and nothing else.

The other half of my approach is less exciting. I keep enough cash on hand that a surprise bill never forces me to sell something at a bad moment. A large, depreciating asset is exactly the kind of thing that produces surprise bills.

I learned the charter side the expensive way in Antibes in 2019. It was 7am on the quay and I had a signed €185,000 contract in my hand. Since then I read the fine print much more carefully than I read the brochure.

Does anyone else here rent instead of owning for the same reasons?

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Is it better to rent or buy a yacht if you only use it a few weeks a year?

For most people who use a yacht only a few weeks a year, renting wins, and it isn't close.

Start with what ownership costs. A rough industry rule is that a yacht costs about 10% of its purchase price every year to run. That covers crew wages, berthing, insurance, maintenance, refits and fuel. The costs keep coming whether you sail for three weeks or stay ashore all season. On top of that the boat itself loses value, so you pay for depreciation as well as upkeep.

A charter turns all of that into one cost tied to the weeks you actually use. The owner and the management company deal with crew, repairs and paperwork. You can also pick a different boat and a different cruising area each year, which owners rarely do.

Ownership makes more sense in a few cases: you're on board for a large part of the year, you want a custom boat you can't charter, or you plan to charter it out yourself to cover costs. That last plan rarely covers as much as the broker suggests.

The other half of the decision is liquidity. I keep enough cash that a surprise bill never forces me to sell an asset at a bad time. Big floating assets are exactly the kind of thing that produce surprise bills, and that's another reason renting sits well with a cautious financial plan.

Chartering has its own risks. Read the contract closely, especially cancellation terms, the advance provisioning allowance (APA) and what happens if the yacht has a mechanical problem. I learned that standing on a quay in Antibes in 2019 with a signed €185,000 contract, and I wrote up the lessons here if you want more detail: https://siavashsed.github.io/crestcharter/rent-yachts-own-cash-finance-tips/

In short: if you use it for weeks, rent. If you use it for months, run the numbers on owning.
