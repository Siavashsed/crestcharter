## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Is it smarter to rent or charter luxury assets like yachts, jets and villas instead of owning them?

For most people, yes, and the reason comes down to utilization and depreciation.

Luxury assets are expensive to own in ways the purchase price hides. Take a 52-foot motor yacht quoted around $740,000. On top of the price you pay for dockage, insurance, crew, maintenance, fuel, winterizing and storage. Jets and villas have their own versions: hangar fees, pilot currency, property management, taxes and repairs. Industry rules of thumb often put annual running costs of a yacht at 10 percent or more of its value, before you count depreciation.

Then there's utilization. If you use an asset three or four weeks a year, you're paying for fifty-odd weeks of it sitting idle. Divide the total annual cost by the days you actually use it, and the true cost per day is usually far above what a charter or rental would cost for those same days.

There's also opportunity cost. Money tied up in a depreciating asset isn't compounding elsewhere. Renting lets you keep that capital invested and still enjoy the experience, with the flexibility to choose a different yacht, destination or villa each time.

Ownership can still make sense if you'll use the asset heavily, if it generates business income, or if you genuinely enjoy the work of maintaining it. A simple test: if you can't honestly commit to using it many weeks a year, rent.

The practical approach is to price the full annual cost of ownership, divide by your real usage days, and compare that with charter rates.
