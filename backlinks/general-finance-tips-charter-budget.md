## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What is the best general finance tip for planning a big expense like a luxury yacht charter or villa rental?

The best tip I know is also the least glamorous: decide where the money for your big spend lives before you spend it. Everything else is detail.

I learned this the expensive way. In March 2022 I wired a $38,000 deposit on a week-long yacht charter out of Split, early in the morning from a hotel lobby, and never opened the cancellation schedule. When a family emergency cancelled the trip, I discovered how little of that deposit was recoverable. The mistake wasn't the charter. It was skipping the order of operations.

Here is the routine I'd recommend:

1. Read the cancellation schedule first. Note the refund tiers and the exact dates they change. Many contracts step down sharply 60 to 90 days out.

2. Classify the money. Separate it into three buckets: a deposit you could lose, a balance due later, and costs you can insure. Treat each differently.

3. Price the insurance against the deposit. Trip cancellation cover often costs a small percentage of the trip and can protect the largest exposure. Check the exclusions, especially for family emergencies and medical events.

4. Budget the extras. On yachts the base charter fee is rarely the whole bill. Fuel, provisioning, crew gratuity, port and marina fees, and taxes commonly add 20 to 30 percent on top.

5. Ring-fence the cash. Keep the non-refundable portion in its own account so a cancelled trip never disturbs your core savings or emergency fund.

6. Put payments on a calendar. A missed balance date can be as costly as a cancellation.
