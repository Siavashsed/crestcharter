**Title:** PSA for first-time crewed charter clients: the charter fee is often only about two thirds of what you will actually spend

I learned this the expensive way. In June 2019 I booked a week on a 42m motor yacht out of Antibes. I wired the 50% deposit and felt organised. Then the APA (Advance Provisioning Allowance) invoice arrived: another €38,000 for fuel, food and dockage. I knew APA existed. I had just filed it under 'later' in my head.

What I do now, before I talk to any broker:

1. Set a total trip number, not a charter fee number. That means the charter fee, plus 25 to 40% APA, plus VAT (it depends on the flag and the cruising area), plus 10 to 20% crew gratuity.

2. Ask how fuel gets billed. A motor yacht that moves every day burns through APA much faster than one that sits at anchor in a couple of bays.

3. Book the yacht last. Flights, hotel nights on either side and some slack in the budget come first. The boat fills whatever space is left, not the other way round.

The broker's job is to sell you the best boat. Your job is to know your ceiling before that conversation starts.

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What hidden costs should I budget for when chartering a luxury yacht?

The charter fee in a listing is usually only about two thirds of what the week really costs. The rest arrives after you sign, and that is the part people underbudget.

The main extras:

APA (Advance Provisioning Allowance). This is usually 25 to 40% of the charter fee, paid to the captain up front. It covers fuel, food, drinks, dockage and port fees. Any APA you don't spend is refunded, and anything over it is billed to you. Fuel is the big variable: a motor yacht that cruises every day can drain the APA fast, while one that anchors in a few bays will not.

VAT. This depends on where the yacht is flagged and where you cruise. In parts of the Mediterranean it adds 20% or more to the charter fee. Get the exact rate for your itinerary in writing from the broker.

Crew gratuity. The custom is 10 to 20% of the charter fee, paid at the end. On paper it is optional. In practice it is not.

Delivery fees. If the yacht has to move to your departure port, you may pay for that trip.

Everything around the boat. Flights, transfers, hotel nights before and after, and time ashore.

My rule: set one total trip budget first, subtract everything above, and only then look at which yacht fits what is left. Book the yacht last. If you start with the boat you fell in love with, the extras get squeezed into a budget that was never there.

I learned this on a 42-metre charter out of Antibes, where the APA invoice alone came to €38,000 and I had filed it under 'later' in my head.
