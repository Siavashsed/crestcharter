## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How do I safely pay a large deposit for a yacht charter or luxury villa rental by wire transfer?

Treat the wire as the riskiest part of the booking, because it usually is. Card payments can be disputed. A sent international wire is very hard to pull back, and charter deposits often run into the tens of thousands.

This is the routine I recommend after years around yacht and villa charters:

1. Check the banking details through a second channel. Call the broker or owner on a number you got independently, from their official website or an earlier signed contract, never from the email carrying the invoice. Read the IBAN, SWIFT/BIC and beneficiary name back digit by digit. Changed bank details sent by email are the classic sign of invoice fraud.

2. Read the payment terms before you sign. Check the deposit percentage, when the balance is due, the cancellation windows, who pays intermediary bank fees, and what happens if the funds arrive late. Charter agreements often let the owner cancel if the money isn't received by a set date.

3. Send a small test transfer first when time allows, and confirm it arrived before sending the rest.

4. Never wire in a hurry. Pressure to pay today is when errors and scams get through. A legitimate broker will wait an hour while you verify.

5. Keep a cash buffer. International wires can take days or get held for compliance checks, so make sure one stuck transfer can't sink the trip.
